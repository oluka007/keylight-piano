# Code of Conduct

Keylight is a small, volunteer-run project for people who want to learn piano. We want
the issue tracker, pull requests and every other space around it to be a welcoming place
to ask a beginner question and to disagree about code. This is our own short version of
the usual rules.

## What we expect

- Be kind and patient. Many people here are new to programming, to GitHub, or to
  playing an instrument — sometimes all three.
- Assume good faith. Ask what someone meant before deciding they meant the worst.
- Give useful feedback about the work, not the person. "This crashes on Windows" is
  helpful; "this is a mess" is not.
- Respect people's time: search before opening a duplicate issue, keep reports on
  topic, and say thank you when someone helps.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, threats or personal attacks of any kind.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments about someone's identity, background,
  experience level or beliefs.
- Sexual attention or imagery in any project space.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, trolling, or repeatedly reopening a settled
  decision.
- Publishing anyone's private information without consent, or anything illegal.

## Where this applies

Everywhere the project lives: issues, pull requests, commit messages and discussions, and
anywhere else you represent Keylight.

## Reporting a problem

If you see or experience behaviour that breaks these rules, you can:

- Contact the maintainer through the repository owner's GitHub profile
  ([github.com/scizerss](https://github.com/scizerss)), or
- Open an issue titled **Conduct** — but keep it free of personal details and names.
  Describe what happened in general terms, and say you would like to speak privately.

Reports are read by a maintainer and kept as private as we reasonably can. If your report
is about that maintainer, say so and ask who else can look at it.

## What happens next

Maintainers will look at the report in good faith and decide what to do — from a quiet
word, to a warning, to removing a comment or a contribution, to a temporary or permanent
ban, depending on how serious and how repeated the behaviour is. We will not act on a
report just because someone asked us to; we will look at what happened. We may not reply
instantly — this is a volunteer project — but we will take it seriously.
